# Positioning: a narrowed pitch, drafted for comparison

**Status: DRAFT. Nothing here is shipped.** The landing page is unchanged. This exists so the
change can be read against what is live before anyone decides.

Prompted by a strategy review arguing CloakCal should stop selling "a private calendar for
everyone" and start selling to people whose availability is useful but whose life is not
public: independent consultants, therapists, lawyers, recruiters, executives, founders.

---

## What is live today

> **Coming soon**
> # Not everything is for *everyone*.
> CloakCal encrypts your events on your device, then shows each person exactly as much as you
> choose. Your client sees a meeting. Your colleagues see busy. Everyone else sees nothing.

The hero's only control is the live demo — *Show the week as: You / Priya / Marcus / Everyone
else* — with no sign-in button, because sign-ups are closed and the honest call to action is
the product doing its trick.

**This copy is good and most of it should survive.** The headline is the product's whole idea
in five words. The three-clause sub is the same idea made concrete, in the same order the demo
plays it. Nothing below is an argument that the current page is bad.

## What the narrowed version changes

Only the answer to *who is this for*. The mechanism sentence stays; the audience sentence is
new.

> **Coming soon**
> # Not everything is for *everyone*.
> The calendar for people whose availability is useful, but whose life is not public.
>
> CloakCal encrypts your events on your device, then shows each person exactly as much as you
> choose. Your client sees a meeting. Your colleagues see busy. Everyone else sees nothing.

One added line, set at `--text-lead` between the headline and the sub. Everything else on the
page is untouched.

### Why a line rather than a rewrite

The demo already does the persuading, and it is the hero's only control. A visitor who reads
one line before reaching it is being told who the page is for; a visitor made to read a
paragraph is being sold to before they have seen anything work. The review's own strongest
point is that the audience-specific preview is what lands — so the copy's job is to get out of
its way faster, not to say more.

### The honesty band does not change

It is the most trustworthy thing on the site and it is bound by rule 1. Narrowing the audience
does not narrow what the server can read, and any temptation to make the claim sound stronger
for a professional audience is the exact overclaim the band exists to prevent.

---

## What this costs, stated plainly

**It excludes people the product serves.** Someone hiding a therapy appointment from their
employer is not a consultant, and they are the most sympathetic user CloakCal has. A line
naming professionals tells them this is not for them, which is false.

**Nobody can act on it.** Sign-ups are closed and the flag is fail-closed, so the page cannot
convert anyone today. Repositioning now optimises a funnel with no entrance — and if it is
wrong, the cost is paid before there is any evidence to correct it with.

**It is reversible, cheaply.** One paragraph, one token, no structure. That is the argument
for trying it whenever the door opens, and the argument against agonising over it now.

## The alternative worth weighing

Say the *situation* rather than the *job title*:

> For anyone whose calendar says more about them than they meant it to.

Keeps the professional reader — a lawyer reads their own case in it immediately — without
telling the therapy-appointment user the product is not for them. Weaker as targeting, which
is the whole point of the review's advice, so it is a genuine trade rather than a free win.

## Recommendation

**Hold both until sign-ups open**, then ship the narrowed line and watch it, because the
review is right that "a private calendar for everyone" is a weaker sentence than any specific
alternative. The thing worth doing *before* the door opens is not choosing between these
two — it is the usability round the review also asks for, run with the three tasks it names.
Those answers would decide this in an afternoon and cannot be reasoned to from here.
